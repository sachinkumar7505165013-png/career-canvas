Check My Resume — Free How it works

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is mainly a database and search tool that employers use to store, search and rank incoming resumes — not a mysterious gatekeeper silently rejecting resumes for arbitrary reasons. Many formatting "rules" that circulate online are myths; what actually matters more is whether your resume's content realistically matches the language of the job description, and whether a few genuine formatting issues (like multi-column layouts or text inside images) are getting in the way of accurate parsing. This checker looks at both.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is the score?

It's an estimate, not a guarantee of how any specific company's ATS will score your resume — different systems weigh things differently. Treat it as directional guidance, not a pass/fail test.

Will bold text or color break the ATS?

Generally no — this is one of the more common myths. Most modern ATS platforms handle standard bold, italic and color formatting fine. The bigger risks are multi-column layouts and text embedded in images. Our ATS-friendly formatting guide goes into more detail.

Do I need a job description to use this?

You'll get the most useful result with one, since keyword matching is relative to a specific role. Without one, the checker can still flag structural and formatting issues.

Is this free?

Yes, there's no limit on how many times you can check your resume.

Should I just stuff in keywords to raise my score?

No — the goal is describing your real experience using the vocabulary the job is already searching for, not padding your resume with irrelevant terms, which can backfire with a human reviewer.
